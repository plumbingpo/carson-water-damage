Where Water Damage Starts in San Francisco Properties

Some water problems need a phone call this minute, and others can safely wait until morning. The five below are ones we see constantly in San Francisco, listed from most pressing to least.

Bursts: A pipe that has split is the most urgent call there is, because it puts out water for as long as the supply is on. Depending on the season, the break may follow a freeze, and it often shows only when the ice inside melts. Close the main valve, open a faucet to relieve pressure and call right away, even at night. If a ceiling is sagging from the weight of water, stay out from under it and do not poke it.

Runoff: Rain running into the house at ground level ranks a close second. There is no valve to close here, so lift what you can off the floor and avoid wet electrical outlets. Having passed over pavement and soil on the way in, it carries dirt and germs. As soon as it is safe to enter, we pump the water out and take away porous items it ruined.

Kitchens: A dishwasher that leaks from its door or a refrigerator line that drips behind the unit calls for same-day attention. Close the small valve under the sink or behind the appliance and the flow usually stops. What remains is the water trapped below the cabinets and floor covering, and it will not dry by itself. In San Francisco kitchens we often dry cabinets in place by sending air through small holes in the toe kick.

Crawlspaces: Dampness in the space below the floor can wait a few days, though not a few months. Puddles on the ground cover, dripping ductwork or wet insulation between the joists are what an inspection usually turns up. Left alone, the damp air rises into the rooms above and the wood framing begins to decay. We extract the water, dry the wood to a safe reading and tell you what we think the source is.

Odors: A musty smell near the site of an old leak is the least urgent item here, yet it deserves an inspection soon. Since growth can start 24 to 48 hours after materials get wet, an old leak that was never fully dried is a likely culprit. Do not scrub or spray a large patch yourself, because disturbing it spreads spores through the house. Our technicians test the area, contain it and remove the growth along with the moisture feeding it across San Francisco.

Preventing Water Damage in San Francisco Homes

A large share of the jobs we attend in San Francisco began as a minor fault that could have been caught. A few minutes of checking a couple of times a year catches most of it.

Begin at the hoses. The rubber hoses on washing machines, dishwashers, toilets and ice makers wear out, and braided stainless replacements are inexpensive. Open each sink cabinet and check for water marks, a warped base or a damp odor. Check the water heater for rust at the base and drain a little from the tank once a year to clear sediment. Clean gutters and downspouts that discharge well away from the house keep rain out of the foundation.

Know where your main shutoff valve is and make sure everyone in the house can turn it. Leak sensors placed by the water heater, washer and under sinks cost little and will alert you early. If you find something in your San Francisco home that worries you, such as a stain that keeps growing, have it looked at before it becomes a cleanup.

The First Hour After Water Damage in San Francisco

People mean well in the first hour after a leak, and some of what they do makes things worse. Here are the slip-ups we run into most on San Francisco jobs, with the better option for each.

Do not use a household vacuum on water. You risk a shock and a dead vacuum, and a mop with old towels does the job safely. Do not turn on ceiling fans or lights under a wet ceiling, and stay out of rooms where the ceiling is sagging. Leave fitted carpet in place, because pulling it up the wrong way can stretch or rip a carpet we might have saved. Do not turn the heat up high to dry things out, because warm, damp air is what mold likes.

Do shut off the water, open cabinet doors and closets so air can reach them, and hang up wet fabrics and cushions to drip. Do take pictures before moving anything. Do keep children and pets away from the area. Most of all, phone early, so a Carson Restoration crew is already heading for San Francisco while you work through this list.

How Drying Works on a San Francisco Water Damage Job

A common question from San Francisco customers is what to expect once the machines are running. On day one, the crew extracts the water, removes anything that cannot be dried, and sets air movers and dehumidifiers in the affected rooms. Expect noise on the level of a few big fans, plus noticeable warmth in the room. It needs to stay on around the clock, and turning it off, shifting it or airing out the room slows everything down.

Each day after that, a technician visits to take moisture readings in the same spots and record them. Equipment is repositioned toward the slower spots as others finish, and surplus units leave the house. The technician shares the readings and explains the trend.

A typical job takes 3 to 5 days of drying. Longer jobs happen when the leak went unnoticed, when insulation or hardwood got soaked, or when more than one level is wet. When every reading matches the dry standard, Carson Restoration removes the equipment and gives you the drying record for your San Francisco property. Only then should rebuilding start, because closing up damp walls traps the problem inside.
