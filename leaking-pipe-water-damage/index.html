Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Carson: What to Know

A leaking pipe rarely announces itself. Water seeps out behind drywall, at the back of a sink cabinet or under the concrete until a stain, a soft floor or an unexplained water bill gives it away. By then the materials around the pipe have often been wet for weeks. For Carson homes, our crew maps where the water traveled and dries the hidden damage along with the visible damage.

Fixing the pipe ends the leak, yet the moisture already inside the wall or floor stays put. That is our part of the job. Our crew maps the damp materials with thermal imaging and moisture meters, opens up no more than necessary, and runs dehumidifiers and air movers until the readings return to normal. A slow leak gives mold plenty of time to begin, so we inspect for growth too and treat any surface it has reached.

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut Off the WaterFor a leak at a toilet or sink, turn off the small supply valve feeding that fixture. If you cannot find the source, turn off the main shutoff valve for the house.
  2. Keep Power AwayTurn off the breaker serving the area when water is close to a light fixture, switch or outlet. Do not touch wet electrical devices or cords.
  3. Check the Water MeterMake sure nothing in the house is using water and read the meter. If the dial is still moving, a pipe is leaking somewhere you cannot see, possibly under the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographClear out the cabinet or closet near the leak and take photos of stains, warped materials and the pipe itself before anything is repaired or thrown away.
  5. Get a Moisture CheckBefore the opening is sealed, get the surrounding materials checked with a moisture meter. Drywall can feel dry to the touch while the cavity behind it is still damp.
Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Leaking Pipe Water Damage

A slow leak and a sudden one are not handled the same way by insurance. Policies often cover damage that is sudden and accidental. A leak lasting weeks or months usually falls outside that, and certain policies name it as an exclusion. If the leak was concealed and you could not have known, it may be viewed more favorably than a visible leak nobody dealt with. Your agent and the policy itself are the places to confirm. We document the source of the leak, moisture readings and photos so the adjuster has clear facts.

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Failed solder joints or crimp rings hidden inside wall and ceiling cavities.
  • Water pressure set too high, which wears on every joint and valve until a slow weep begins.
  • Pipes in exterior walls or crawl spaces that crack after a freeze and seep once they thaw.
  • Loose or worn compression fittings and supply connections under sinks and behind toilets.
  • Fasteners from remodeling work or wall hangings that nick or pierce a line inside the wall.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Ruined Cabinet Bases

Sink cabinet bottoms are usually particleboard, which swells and crumbles when it soaks up a leak. Because the board never shrinks back, a long leak often ends with a new cabinet base.

Hidden Mold Growth

Nothing suits mold better than the dark, moist space inside a wall. With a slow leak it often covers the back of the drywall before a single spot shows on the painted side.

Decayed Wall Framing

Wall studs and bottom plates that stay damp for weeks begin to soften and decay. Any piece too weak to carry load gets cut out and swapped for new lumber.

Ceiling and Drywall Damage

Brown water rings and blistered paint show up when a line drips behind a wall or over a ceiling. If the board keeps taking on water it loses its stiffness, starts to sag and may have to be removed.
